Single scale

large displacement → poor local linearization
Lucas–Kanade assumes the displacement is small enough for a local first-order approximation. Large inter-frame motion can move the correct match outside the useful local neighborhood, causing wrong or lost tracks.

Multi scale

16 px → 8 px → 4 px → 2 px
Downsampling shrinks apparent displacement. Estimate motion first at a coarse resolution, propagate it upward, then refine at finer scales. This preserves the small-motion assumption much better.
Teaching takeaway: multi-scale estimation does not change the physical motion; it changes the image scale. The same large full-resolution displacement becomes a much smaller displacement at coarse resolution, making classical correspondence estimation easier.
