Optical Flow vs Feature Tracking vs Lucas–Kanade

CS4243 · Tracking

The same synthetic motion is used in all three panels. Optical flow describes motion at many image locations between nearby frames; feature tracking follows selected, distinctive image features across many frames; Lucas–Kanade (LK) is a local patch-alignment algorithm that can provide the frame-to-frame displacement used by either task.

1 · Optical flow

Many locations, one pair of nearby frames.

Output: a motion vector at each sampled location.

2 · Feature tracking

One selected image feature, repeatedly followed across frames.

Output: a trajectory with persistent feature identity.

3 · Lucas–Kanade alignment

Local method: align the eye patch near its previous location.

LK estimates a small displacement Δp.
Key distinction: LK is not a third tracking task. It is a local solver. Apply it at many locations → sparse optical flow. Reapply it to the same selected feature over time → feature tracking.
Optical flow
many locations · usually two nearby frames · no persistent identity is required.
Feature tracking
selected image features · multiple frames · identity is maintained to form trajectories.
Relation: feature tracking is a common form of point tracking, where the tracked points are chosen for distinctive local appearance (e.g. corners or textured patches).
Lucas–Kanade
one local patch update · assumes small motion and a good initialization.
