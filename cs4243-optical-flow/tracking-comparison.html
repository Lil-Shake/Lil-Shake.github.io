Optical Flow, Feature Tracking, and Lucas–Kanade

All three panels use the same synthetic translation of the same face. The demo separates what is estimated (optical flow vs. a feature trajectory) from how a local displacement can be estimated (Lucas–Kanade alignment).

1 · Optical flow

Estimate motion at many image locations between a pair of nearby frames.

Output: a field of frame-to-frame motion vectors. Persistent identity across a long sequence is not the main output.

2 · Feature tracking

Choose a distinctive image feature and follow the same feature over multiple frames.

Output: a trajectory with persistent feature identity. The selected point is represented by the local image patch around it.

3 · Lucas–Kanade alignment

A local alignment method: start near the previous position and estimate a small displacement Δp.

Method: local patch alignment from frame t to t+1.
Key relationship
LK at many selected locations→sparse optical flow
repeat LK on the same selected feature→feature trajectory
Feature tracking vs. point tracking. Feature tracking is a common form of point tracking in which the tracked points are chosen because their local image appearance is distinctive—for example corners or textured patches. Here, “feature” means an image-space feature, not a neural-network latent feature.
